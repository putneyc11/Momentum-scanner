# HYP-002 — the tuner's stop bounds excluded the region where the edge lives

**Status:** building
**Date:** 2026-08-28  **Pod:** redgreen, reclaim (search space shared with flag, igniter, gapgo)

## Claim

**Restated 2026-08-28 after Red Team killed HYP-001.** The original claim rested
on two measurements that no longer hold, and it is rewritten here rather than
quietly patched.

*What it said:* redgreen's entry beats random by 6.6x at 5 minutes, yet it
backtests below 1.0 because 83% of trades exit on the stop; widening the stop
box to reach 12 / 30 / 90, where it scores 1.16, should let the tuner find that.

*Why that is dead:* `research/edge.js` drew its random control uniformly across
09:30–19:30 while 6,191 of redgreen's 13,870 signals fire in the 09:00 hour, so
the 6.6x was largely a comparison of intraday regimes. Controlled to the same
15-minute bucket, the signal scores **worse** than its control. And the 0.70 →
1.16 sweep in `research/stops2.js` scored all six stop triples on the full 144
days and both halves before the best row was picked, so every day later called
"test" was already spent. Both in `REVIEWS/HYP-001.md`.

*What is left, and it is narrower:* **the tuner's stop ceiling was never chosen
on evidence, and the region above it has never been searched.** `stopAtrMult 3 /
minStopPct 4 / maxStopPct 12` was an assumption nobody tested. This hypothesis
now claims only that the region is worth searching, and it resolves that with a
measurement no prior search has read.

## Category

(c) parameter range — `WIDE_STOP_RANGES` in `lib/strategies.js`, spread into
`redgreen` and `reclaim`. No entry logic, no discovery gate, no fill model.

## Mechanism

On a $1–5 name a 1–4% stop is roughly one bar of ordinary range, so a position
can be stopped out by noise before anything the entry selects for has time to
appear. A wider stop lets the trade survive its own volatility.

**But a win here would not be a win for that story, and the report must not
claim it is.** Raising `maxStopPct` only lifts a *cap*; the `minStopPct` floor
and the ATR distance still set the stop. At 12 / 30 / 90, 87.5% of exits are
VWAP or time exits rather than stops (1,012 + 779 of 2,046) — the configuration
is not "redgreen with more noise tolerance", it is **redgreen with signal-based
exits**. So the exit-reason mix for any winning champion is reported alongside
the score, and a win is described as a result about exit policy.

## Falsifier

Written before any tune was run, and unchanged by the HYP-001 kill — this is the
measurement that was always going to decide it.

1. **The widened box does not beat the original box on the frozen holdout.**
   Four 200-iteration tunes, identical seeds and cost model, differing only in
   the bounds: widened@11, widened@29, original@11, original@29. If the widened
   arm does not beat the original arm on `bestScore.test` for redgreen on BOTH
   seeds, the region was not reachable-and-valuable, it was just reachable.
   (One-sided — see the handicap note below. A loss is not a verdict on the
   region, only on the search's ability to cross it in 200 iterations.)
2. **Validate climbs while the holdout does not.** The holdout is scored twice,
   after the search ends, and gates nothing. If validate improves and test does
   not, the search found the validation split, not an edge, and the wider box
   made that easier rather than harder by giving it more room to overfit.

Unlike the sweep this replaces, no arm here has seen the holdout: the split is
frozen in `lib/tune.js`, `test` is scored exactly twice after the search ends,
and it gates nothing. Two seeds, and an old-box control, because one tuning run
is a sample of one.

reclaim is expected to fail #1 — it peaked at 0.71 across the stop sweep under
tiered slippage, though that figure carries the same contamination as the rest
of `stops2.js` and is a prior, not a finding. It is tuned because the assignment
says to tune it, and because a negative result recorded is worth more than a
negative result assumed.

## What this cannot answer

Two limits inherited from the machinery, stated so nobody reads past them:

- **Backtest profit factor is not an estimate of the traded system.** The
  backtest sizes off the next bar's open and volume; the live loop sizes off the
  signal bar's close and volume.
- **The fill assumption is load-bearing and this does not test it.**
  `MAX_BAR_PARTICIPATION` is frozen at 10% and is deliberately not overridable,
  so nothing here establishes that these names absorb that size. Red Team's
  sensitivity on the swept config — PF 1.16 at 10%, 1.12 at 5%, 1.05 at 2%,
  1.00 at 1% — is the shape of that risk, and it applies to whatever this finds.

## Cost

None to discovery — this adds and removes no ranked movers. The cost is search
density: at a fixed 200 iterations the box for three knobs is roughly 4–8x
larger per axis, so each iteration covers less ground. If the wider arm loses,
that is one of the two candidate explanations and it is cheap to separate by
raising `--iters`.

## Implementation

Two commits, deliberately in this order, because they measure different things.

**`cefc472` — the widened shared tables.** `RANGES` in `lib/strategy.js` and
`MGMT_RANGES` in `lib/strategies.js`, all three stop keys:

```
stopAtrMult [0.8, 3, 0.1] → [0.8, 15, 0.1]
minStopPct  [1, 4, 0.5]   → [1, 40, 0.5]
maxStopPct  [4, 12, 1]    → [4, 95, 1]
```

This is what runs A and B measure. It reaches five pods, which is why it is not
the merge candidate.

**`865059a` — contained to the two pods that were swept.** The shared boxes go
back to 3 / 4 / 12; the wide bounds move into a named `WIDE_STOP_RANGES` and are
spread into `redgreen` and `reclaim` alone. **This is the config intended for
merge**, and run E measures it.

Bounds only, in both. No winning value is hardcoded and no `DEFAULTS` move, so
neither commit changes any pod's behaviour on its own — a range is only a search
bound, and saved champions are untouched until a pod is retuned.

Ceilings sit above the measured corner (12 / 30 / 90) deliberately: a bound
placed exactly at the best tested value pins the optimum to the wall and teaches
you nothing about what is past it. `maxStopPct` stops at 95 rather than 100
because at 100 the stop sits at zero, which is not a wide stop, it is no stop.

`RIDE_RANGES` (moon, surge) was never widened and a test pins that.

### Why contained rather than global

- **gapgo's wide-stop gain is a cost artefact.** `research/falsifier2.js`: +0.14
  profit factor with costs, −0.16 with costs removed. Its entry is worse than
  random, so a wider stop buys it fewer round trips and a flattering number with
  nothing underneath.
- **flag and igniter are having their entries replaced** (HYP-003). Tuning stops
  for a signal about to be deleted produces champions void on arrival.
- **A wider box on a pod with no entry edge yields a flattering champion with
  nothing underneath it.** When this was written the mechanism was concrete:
  `engine.js` promoted on the validate score alone, unattended, every night.
  `3d87d7b` has since deleted the nightly self-tune and split `params/` (in git,
  what trades) from `state/params/` (gitignored, what `engine.js tune` writes),
  so promotion is now a human commit and that specific path to production is
  closed. The argument for containment survives it: nothing should be tuned
  wide without its own clean measurement, and after the HYP-001 kill no pod has
  one — including the two here, which is what the A/B is for.

Three tests pin the containment: both shared tables must keep their original
ceilings, and gapgo, flag and igniter must not cover the wide region. Without
them the next person to spread `MGMT_RANGES` silently undoes this.

## Two things that bias the reading — stated before the numbers exist

### 1. The dead region handicaps the widened arm

The wide box overlaps the shared `maxStopPct` range, so the tuner can draw
`minStopPct 40` alongside `maxStopPct 4`. `stopDist` applies the max clamp last,
so the clamp wins and that pair behaves identically to `minStopPct 1 /
maxStopPct 4`.

Roughly 18% of the widened box is incoherent this way. Critically, those draws
alias to a **narrow** stop — the configuration already known to score 0.64–0.70 —
so they score badly and get rejected. They are wasted iterations, not a trap
that captures the optimum. The search is handicapped, not misled.

Which fixes the direction of the inference in advance:

- **If the wide box wins anyway, the result is stronger than it looks** — it won
  while spending part of its budget on draws that could not win.
- **If the wide box loses, we cannot conclude the box is bad** — only that the
  search could not navigate it at 200 iterations. The falsifier below is
  therefore one-sided, and a loss should be retested at higher `--iters` before
  the region is written off.

The measured dead-draw fraction is reported with the results. If it is
materially above 18%, the handicap is worse than estimated and a coherence
constraint on the pair earns its own hypothesis.

`stopDist` was deliberately not changed to reject the pair: that is an
exit-engine behaviour change, out of scope for a range widening, and it would
move every pod's numbers. It is pinned by test so it reads as known.

### 2. Cross-pollination blurs attribution in the shared-table arm

`lib/tune.js` takes `seeds: siblings` — each pod's search is cross-pollinated
with the other six champions' params, lifting a sibling's value for a knob 25%
of the time instead of taking a random step. In runs A and B the shared tables
are wide, so a wide-stop champion found by flag or gapgo can be seeded straight
into redgreen's search. **redgreen's number in the widened arm is therefore not
purely attributable to redgreen's own box.**

Runs C and D have the same structure with narrow tables, so the A/B contrast is
internally consistent and still answers "does a wider box help." But the clean
attribution lives in run E, where only redgreen and reclaim carry the wide box
and no sibling has a wide stop to donate.
