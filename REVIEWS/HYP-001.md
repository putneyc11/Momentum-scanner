## Defects

1. **FATAL — `research/edge.js` still does not control time of day, despite saying it does.** Commit `e4ae3f1` correctly changes the horizon from bar count to wall-clock time (`research/edge.js:20-42`), but it leaves the control pool unchanged: every bar in the entire 09:30-19:30 entry window is eligible and the draw is uniform from that pool (`research/edge.js:69-79`). Redgreen is not uniform: 6,191 of 13,870 raw signals occur in the 09:00 hour. Under the fixed horizon, the published comparison is +58.45 bps signal versus +7.3 bps uniform-window random. Replacing that control with the exact expected return from the same symbol/day and same hour raises random to +45.89 bps; using the same 15-minute bucket raises it to +87.35 bps, above the signal. The claimed 8.0x entry edge therefore remains mostly an intraday-regime comparison; the hour-matched estimate is roughly 1.27x, and the finer control reverses it. This does not prove redgreen has no edge, but it does invalidate the stated magnitude and any use of this row as a pass/fail gate.

2. **FATAL — the purported frozen holdout was already used to choose `12/30/90`.** `lib/tune.js:68-80` reserves the last 20%, and `lib/tune.js:116-119` touches it only after a tuner search. That protection does not apply to this hypothesis: the committed sweep evaluates all six redgreen stop triples on the full 144 days and on both halves (`research/stops2.js:11-24`), then selects the last/highest row. Those views include every day later called “test.” Thus exactly six committed redgreen candidates were directly scored on the full library, with at least six looks at the nominal holdout; the exact number of exploratory looks before the committed script is unrecoverable. The tiered-cost read on the last 29 days is PF 1.10, but it is a contaminated, post-selection number—not an out-of-sample estimate. The HYP result table is still empty, so there is no independent tune run to rescue the claim.

3. **SERIOUS — the result is fill-cap sensitive and requires aggressive participation.** The backtest allows one order to take 10% of the next minute's entire tape (`lib/backtest.js:98-106`), a threshold the source itself calls “generous” and “the loosest number that is still honest” (`lib/strategy.js:168-178`). At `12/30/90`, 385 of 2,046 entries (18.8%) hit that cap; median participation is 4.83% and the 90th percentile is effectively 10%. Replaying with identical logic and tiered slippage gives PF 1.16 at 10%, 1.12 at 5%, 1.05 at 2%, and 1.00 at 1%. The claimed profitability disappears under a conservative participation assumption. No quote/ask or order-book evidence establishes that these names can absorb the modeled sizes at the modeled price.

4. **SERIOUS — live and backtest sizing are not the same execution model.** The backtest fills at the next bar's open, recomputes risk from that fill, and caps quantity using the next bar's volume (`lib/backtest.js:90-107`). Live sizes from the signal bar's close, `sig.risk`, and the signal bar's volume before the order fills (`engine.js:733-749`). A gap and a volume change between bars therefore alter risk and capacity in opposite code paths. This did not create the `edge.js` result, but it prevents the backtest PF from being an exact estimate of the traded implementation.

5. **SERIOUS — the wall-clock fix is incomplete at the entry and missing-sample boundaries.** `research/edge.js:35-41` still defines entry as the next *array* bar even when it is not the next minute. Of 13,870 raw redgreen signals, 1,310 use a delayed entry bar; the backtest deletes that pending order when the immediately following minute has no bar (`lib/backtest.js:91-98`). The new forward-return rule drops samples with no later print inside the window (`research/edge.js:32-41`). Running the same rule drops 2.1% of redgreen signals versus 3.6% of its random arm. Applying identical code is not identical selection when signal density predicts whether a sample survives. Reporting the aggregate rates is useful, but it does not repair or condition the comparison.

6. **SERIOUS — `maxStopPct:999` is not a no-stop falsifier.** Stop distance is the maximum of ATR distance, `minStopPct`, and structural distance, merely capped by `maxStopPct` (`lib/strategies.js:91-96`). Raising only the cap from 90 to 999 therefore leaves the 30% floor/ATR stop in place; the tiered-cost replay is essentially identical at `12/30/90` and `12/30/999`. A genuine near-no-stop run (`stopAtrMult:0`, `minStopPct:maxStopPct:99`) scores PF 1.20 full-sample versus 1.16 for `12/30/90`, though it falls to 0.95 versus 1.10 on the contaminated last 29 days. The correct conclusion is narrower: 90% is not the operative stop bound, and 87.5% of wide-stop exits are VWAP/time exits (1,012 VWAP + 779 time out of 2,046). This is a different, mostly signal-exit strategy, not simply the original strategy with a little more noise tolerance.

7. **SERIOUS — the backfill biases are unresolved and the polling direction is not measured.** The universe omits subsequently delisted assets (`lib/backfill.js:30-35`), which is optimistic for small-cap momentum and cannot be bounded from this library. Replay evaluates 960 one-minute timestamps while live discovery polls every 90 seconds (`lib/backfill.js:36-40`): that is 50% more polling opportunities, but the PF direction is not logically guaranteed because extra names can be winners, losers, or consume scarce slots. Calling both effects optimistic without a matched live-day comparison overstates what is known. The magnitude of survivorship bias and the actual cadence delta are unidentifiable from the present files.

8. **SERIOUS — a nightly champion that fails holdout is still saved, traded, and risk-weighted.** The tune loop keeps test out of the within-run search (`lib/tune.js:103-119`), but `engine.js:94-102` promotes solely on validate. `engine.js:103-120` then assigns risk from validate while test is only formatted as `DID NOT HOLD`, and the unattended job adopts both params and allocation nightly (`engine.js:437-443`). A holdout that changes no action is telemetry, and there is no enforced reader.

9. **MINOR — three parameters moved together against only one 144-day library.** The sweep changes `stopAtrMult`, `minStopPct`, and `maxStopPct` as a bundled diagonal (`research/stops2.js:14-20`), so it does not identify which knob matters or interactions off that path. The third chronological cut is directionally supportive under tiered costs—PF progresses from 0.70/0.72/0.66 at default to 1.19/1.14/1.16 at `12/30/90` across 48/48/48 days—but all three thirds were visible during selection and are not independent confirmation.

## Holdout design ruling

A single post-search promote/reject bit is materially less contaminating than steering 200 candidate steps, but reusing the same last-20% window every night turns it into another validation set. At seven pods per night, the system can consume up to seven adaptive bits nightly—about 210 decisions per month—while a 20% chronological holdout grows by only about 0.2 trading day per night. `MIN_HOLDOUT_DAYS = 10` is only a startup-size guard (`lib/tune.js:64-80`); it does not make repeated reuse independent, and library growth does not outrun the information spent.

Do not add `heldOut` to the current nightly save condition and call the fixed last 20% unbiased. The better design is an epochal walk-forward promotion gate: freeze one shadow candidate per pod, accumulate a genuinely new quarantine block that neither search nor prior promotion decisions have read, promote once after a preset number of fresh days/trades and a preset improvement margin, then roll that spent block into training and begin a new quarantine block. Until enough fresh evidence accrues, keep the incumbent live. This spends the holdout once and makes the answer enforceable rather than depending on a human reading a log. If nightly adaptation must remain, rename test as reused validation telemetry and automatically quarantine/rollback `DID NOT HOLD`; do not describe it as unbiased.
